| XTPU-0074 | open | low | s | XTPU-0071 | The snapshot validator restates `runtime-snapshot.schema.json` as hand-written property allowlists (`lib/runtime-snapshot-schema-validator.js`), and the equivalence fuzz test cannot detect a *new* optional property: it compares the two against generated documents, and a field neither side has seen is not one a fuzzer reaches. Adding `profiles[].reason` to the schema and not to the allowlist made the deployed applet reject every snapshot the runtime published — reported to the user as "no runtime service is publishing state", indistinguishable from a service that is down. | XTPU-0075 | open | medium | s | XTPU-0074 | Deployment order is part of the snapshot contract and nothing states it. The validator is a closed record, so a field the installed applet does not know invalidates the *whole* snapshot, and the applet reports that as "no runtime service is publishing state" — indistinguishable from a dead service. Observed live on 2026-08-10: after the service began publishing `inputs`, the installed applet rejected every snapshot until the new payload was installed, and the desktop read as if the runtime had stopped. The field was optional and the applet commit landed first, and neither helped, because what matters is the order the two are *deployed*. Options: tolerate unknown top-level properties while still validating the ones it knows (weakens the closed-record guarantee), or state the ordering requirement where an installer will read it and have `omnitensor-verify-install` check the installed applet's contract against the published snapshot. |
